How We Lost Mishthi

Our most perfect user came every few days for 10 weeks. Then we broke — and never reached back. This is how we win her back, stronger.

The one-paragraph truth

Mishthi (20, Goa) didn't leave because she was bored or dissatisfied. She was a daily-rhythm, deeply-vulnerable, habit-forming user — the exact person this product exists for. Her final session shows zero exit signals. We lost her to downtime during her peak engagement, and then to our own silence: nothing personal ever reached out to bring her back. The daily horoscope cron kept firing generic notifications into the void. The habit, fragile at ten weeks, snapped — and there was no thread to pull her home.

Not "when will I marry" — but identity, drugs, situationships, career fear. That is profound trust. The app became her confidant.

"working at 19 earning so good, now in goa... but why don't I feel fulfilled"

The warm, sassy "darling/Scorpio" voice matched her Gen-Z energy. She came back partly for the vibe of a friend who talks back.

Astrology as a mirror = feeling seen

The chart gave her permission and structure to explore herself. "You crave recognition" made her feel understood. That is the real magic — astrology as a self-reflection scaffold.

4 · Where we failed her

1. Downtime in her peak (the killer)

Habits at ~10 weeks are fragile. A broken cue-routine-reward loop during formation can kill a habit permanently. With no streak, no saved history she valued, no social ties — the cost of not returning was zero.

2. We never reached back

After she drifted, the only outreach was a generic daily horoscope push. No personal, thread-aware win-back. We had her phone + push token and used neither meaningfully.

3. Jargon over specifics

She repeatedly begged for concrete answers; we gave planetary mechanics.

"Be more concise" · "Tell me specific, not day-wise" · "I don't understand"

"With your Moon Mahadasha and Rahu Antardasha... leverage those Raj Yogas."

4. No memory, no follow-through

Every session restarted. We never asked "Did you take the Swiggy role? Did you leave Goa? How did the investment go?" A real companion tracks your life. We forgot her every time.

5. Reliability bug: duplicated messages

Her messages & replies repeat 4–6× identically in the data — a real bug that made the experience feel janky and broke the spell. Fix before any win-back.

6. Passive, never proactive

It waited to be asked. It never initiated, never held her accountable to the goals she set, never celebrated a win. Companions check in. We didn't.

5 · The perfect product, built for Mishthi

Reframe: Aurogram is not an astrology app. For Mishthi it is a personal life companion that speaks the language of astrology. Build these seven pillars:

I. Memory & continuity — "it knows me"

A living model of her life threads (the Goa decision, the job, the relationships, the stocks, the fulfillment quest). Every session opens with continuity: "Last time you were weighing the Swiggy role — what happened?"

II. Proactive check-ins — "it follows up"

Replace generic horoscopes with personalised nudges tied to her threads and chart timing: "You wanted to learn stocks — your favourable window opens late May. Want a plan?" Gentle accountability.

III. Specificity on demand — "it actually helps"

Two-layer answers: the why (astrology vibe) + the what (3 concrete options, a next step today, even links). A "get concrete" toggle. Never end on jargon when she asked "what exactly do I do."

IV. Emotional attunement — "a real friend"

Detect vulnerability (drugs, breakups, low motivation, crises) and respond as friend first, astrologer second. Keep the warmth; deepen the empathy and safety.

V. Reliability & grace — "it never abandons me"

Uptime is a feature. When things break: status transparency, and an automatic warm welcome-back that recovers her thread. Fix the duplication bug. Trust is the moat.

VI. Ritual & streaks — "a daily habit"

A designed morning "cosmic check-in" personalised to her goals + a gentle streak + an evening reflection. Reward consistency emotionally, not with cheap gamification.

VII. Modality that fits the person

Mishthi is text-rich; our other north-star user (59029414) is voice-first. The companion should adapt to each person's preferred way of talking — text threads for some, voice for others.

6 · The win-back playbook (for Mishthi, specifically)

Prerequisite: fix the duplication bug and confirm reliability first. Never win a user back into a broken room.

Step 1 — The apology + thread-recovery message (push + SMS)

"Mishthi, it's HolyCow. We had some rough days a while back and I think I left you hanging — I'm sorry. Last time you were figuring out the Swiggy role and whether to leave Goa. I've been sitting with your chart. Can I catch you up?"

Does three things at once: owns the downtime, proves memory, reopens the exact loop she left on.

Step 2 — A "while you were gone" personalised digest

Tie it to her real questions: the stock-learning window she asked about (late May), the Oct-2026 job she joked about, the fulfillment thread. Make returning immediately rewarding.

Step 3 — Lead with the specifics she never got

Deliver the concrete answer she kept asking for: a real career shortlist for her, one next step for today. Show the product changed.

Step 4 — Re-establish the ritual

Invite her to a daily morning check-in built around her goals (job by Oct, learn stocks, find fulfillment). Start a streak that means something.

Step 5 — Close her open loops over the next weeks

Follow through on the job decision, the Goa move, the relationships, the investment — the continuity she never had. This is what converts a returning user into a permanent one.

7 · How we'll know it's working

  • Win-back: does Mishthi reply to step 1, and return for 2+ sessions?
  • Habit: D1 / D7 / D30 return rate (today ~22% / ~16% / churn-heavy).
  • Depth: messages per session (she averaged ~4–5; raise it).
  • Specificity satisfaction: rate of "be more specific" follow-ups (drive to ~0).
  • Continuity: % of sessions that reference a prior thread.
  • Resilience: return rate of users who experience an outage (the metric that failed Mishthi).

The goal isn't to fix one churned user. It's to make the product so reliable, so personal, and so genuinely helpful that the next Mishthi never has a reason — or a chance — to drift. Win her back, and you've learned how to keep all of them.
